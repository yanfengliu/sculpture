# Decisions the record leaves open

Each entry: the question, the choice, the reason and the evidence, with the date. A decision is revisited only with new evidence; the entry then records the change and keeps the old choice.

## Engine

- **Cell size: 8 mm uniform for the first working version** (2026-10-11). Evidence: `engine-options.md` section 2 measures 25.8 M cells and about 610 skin chunks at 8 mm, with fingers at 2.5 cells and eyes and nose readable; 5 mm quadruples memory, remesh and undo cost. The note's 4 mm refinement boxes for the head and hands are the upgrade path once the base carving is judged against the reference photographs; they are not built first because the seam between resolutions is a cost with no evidence yet that the base is insufficient. Known consequence: drill holes at 8 mm come out oversized, so the drilling stage either waits for refinement boxes or shows the drill without resolving its holes and says so in its detail.
- **Values are Int16 fixed point (× 256) with a band per grid, not Int8** (2026-10-11). The stock grid keeps a narrow band of 3 cells, so a blow updates only its box dilated by 3 cells; the figure grid keeps a wide band (32 cells at first, 16 since the same day, see below), because the carving rule `max(stock, min(-tool, figure - margin))` needs the figure's distance to be true out to the stage margin plus the stock's band, which a 2-cell band would clamp and the cut would then overrun the margin. Int16 costs twice the memory of the note's Int8 (about 66 MB per grid at 8 mm in the provisional block) and buys one encoding for both grids with no per-grid scale to get wrong. Memory stays far under the page's 1.5 GB budget.
- **Chunks of 32³ cells meshed from 33³ windows by marching cubes with flat shading** (2026-10-11). Measured 0.2 to 0.35 ms per surface chunk single-threaded (`engine-options.md`, section 1). Workers are stateless meshers fed by transferable buffers; they return positions only and the material's `flatShading` derives the normals, which quarters the transfer. Marching cubes rounds a crease over one cell, which reads as a worn edge; dual contouring is the upgrade if the owner wants knife-sharp creases.
- **Backward playback by an undo log, forward jumps by replay** (2026-10-11; superseded for the David by the next entry on 2026-10-14). Each applied blow records the old values of its dilated box (0.5 to 4 KB at 8 mm Int16, 20 to 72 MB for 20,000 blows); step back and play backward restore bytes; a forward jump re-applies blows in index order. No full-grid checkpoints. If a measured jump across the whole making exceeds the 3 s budget, gzipped stage snapshots are the next step.
- **A plan file and snapshots built in Node; the undo log runs from the last snapshot loaded** (2026-10-14, engine-b; the snapshot fallback the entry above names). Evidence: on 2026-10-14 the 29,209-blow plan, planned in the page with 8 workers, took 53.5 s for a jump from 0 to N (planning about 30 s of it; Node plans the same steps in 32.5 s single-threaded), 2.7 s back to 0, up to 9.9 s for a random jump and 56 s to ready for a load at `t = N`; the page also stalled at step boundaries while it planned the next step and swept. Choice: `scripts/build-plan.mjs` plans once in Node and writes the blows and each step's swept cells (2.6 MB) and 58 snapshots (185 MB gzipped in all, 0.4 to 3.9 MB each, 12 to 20 MB inflated: the band cells only, stored as the stock less the figure, which is 0 or the step's margin wherever a pass stopped on its allowance) at blow 0, at every step's end and 36 inside the ten steps whose replay would exceed about half a second on the pool. With them the same protocol measured 1.65 to 2.3 s, 1.5 to 1.8 s, at most 2.1 to 2.7 s, and 2.1 to 2.7 s to ready with a warm shader cache. Segments of about a second (33 snapshots, 106 MB) left the slowest random jump at 3.1 s under the other worktrees' load, hence half a second: disk for margin. The undo log runs from the last snapshot loaded rather than over the current segment only, so playing backward after playing forward never waits for a load; memory stays as before (175 MB of undo after a play from 0 to N). Known consequence, and why the page takes the plan from the file rather than checking its own against it: Chrome 155's V8 computes `Math.sin`, `cos`, `tan`, `atan2`, `exp`, `log`, `acos`, `asin` and `cbrt` differently in the last bit from Node 24's V8 13.6 (`hypot`, `pow` and `sqrt` agree; 200,000 seeded inputs each, 2026-10-14), so a generator that uses them plans differently in the page: the in-page plan matches Node's to step 12 and differs from `pupils-and-nostrils` on (digest `4e800a79` against `c2b57b0c` at t = N). The carving uses only `tan` and `cos` of the tools' fixed angles, which agree, so the page's own replay of the file's plan reproduced every snapshot byte for byte (`npm run model:verify`, which goes red when a snapshot's digest differs); with the file every browser shows the one plan Node built. The plan file's header binds it to what it was built with: the planner's fingerprint, the snapshot format, the grid, the block and the stock band.
- **Planning is step by step from the state the previous steps leave** (2026-10-11). A step's generator runs once, when the making first needs it, from the stock as the earlier steps left it; the result is cached, so the blows at any `t` are the same whatever the history. `planAll()` forces the whole plan for tests and for the plan digest.
- **WebGLRenderer with GLSL injection, not WebGPURenderer** (2026-10-11). Headless capture on this machine gets the GPU either way, but CI has only software WebGL, and the fleet's capture tooling is WebGL (`engine-options.md`, section 6). The material and renderer sit behind one module so a WebGPU port stays contained.

## Time and scale

- **Playback is a time-lapse and says so** (2026-10-11). About 20,000 shown blows over about five minutes at 1×: the mallet strikes about four times a second, each visible strike applying a short run of planned blows along the stroke's path, and each shown blow stands for the number of real blows its step records (`blowsPerShown`, shown in the label). Reason: the real making was millions of blows over nearly three years; a real rhythm would take hours to watch, and a faster mallet would blur. The chips shown are the size the technique note documents for the tool; the bite shown per blow is larger than a real blow's, which is the one compromise, stated in the page's detail text.
- **The simulated calendar runs from 13 September 1501 to 8 September 1504** (2026-10-11), with the milestones `david-history.md` documents between them (the February 1502 report of half done; the move in May 1504). Each step's calendar span is set so the stages' share of the time matches what the record supports, and the remaining spans are the coordinator's interpolation, marked as such in the step details.
- **A bulk crater has a ragged rim and a pitted floor** (2026-10-14, engine-a): one shown bulk blow stands for about a thousand real pits, and tools.md (Subbia, The mark) describes their field as a pitted, crater-like crust with 10 to 20 mm pits, which a smooth flat-floored cylinder did not show. The crater's radius now wobbles around its axis by up to 20 percent (harmonics of 2, 3 and 5 lobes at 10, 7 and 3 percent of the radius, at phases drawn from the blow's own point, direction and size), and its floor and walls carry conical pits 11 to 18 mm across and up to 5 mm deep, one per 22 mm cube of a lattice fixed in the stone and turned off the block's axes, each pit's radius and centre drawn from its cell so the pits fall irregularly (`src/stock/tool-volumes.ts`, `src/stock/tool-frame.ts`). The pits are half as deep as a real 90-degree pit of their width, because their slope adds to the distance's Lipschitz constant, by which the carving loop widens its shell (1.87 against the plain cylinder's 1); at 8 mm cells a pit spans about two cells, so it reads as an irregular dimpling of the floor and walls, and the marble shader's point texture adds the finer pitting. The pits belong to the stone, not to a blow: two shown craters that overlap at one depth stand for the same real pits, and pits drawn per blow rewrote the shared floor's band and cost 28 percent more undo per bulk blow (`src/stock/undo-budget.test.ts`). A crater removes on average about 2 percent more than the plain cylinder, which `closedFormVolume` counts. Cost, measured as CPU time on the synthetic figure at 8 mm: a bulk blow takes about twice as long to apply (8 to 10 ms against 3 to 4 ms on a flat face), the plan's replay about a quarter longer and its planning about a fifth.

## Block

- **The block is 2.22 m wide, 5.25 m tall and 1.54 m deep** (2026-10-11; superseding the same day's 2.04 × 5.25 × 1.40 provisional). Height is the one recorded dimension, nine braccia (`david-history.md`, "The block and what it forced"). Width and depth are not recorded; the statue's published width is 1.99 m, but the fetched mesh measures 2.158 m wide and 1.489 m deep at 5.17 m tall (the pipeline's normalised box), and the block must hold the figure, so each is the measured extent plus about 3 cm of stone per side, which the roughing removes. The figure stands on the block's bottom face, centred on its footprint, running the block's full height, which leaves about 8 cm at the crown where the sources put the old crust.
- **The figure grid's band is 16 cells, not 32** (2026-10-11). At 32 cells the gzipped 8 mm grid was 31 MB because the band covered most of the figure's box; 16 cells (128 mm) exceeds what the rule needs, the largest stage margin (30 mm) plus the stock's own band (24 mm), so the carving rule never clamps a cut short whatever the bulk bite, and the file roughly halves (a reviewer corrected the earlier justification, which added the cut depth).
- **The real-blows ratio is one inferred rule, computed from the planned count** (2026-10-11, after review): no source gives a blow rate (`tools.md`, Subbia and Mazzuolo), so the rate is an inference (point one a second, toothed chisels one and a half, rubbing tools one stroke a second, five working hours a day over the step's days), the label shows the ratio the engine computes from the step's planned blows, and no detail text states a number. Steps whose count the record gives directly (the knot's one or two blows, a drawn mark, a drilled hole, a brush stroke) state that instead. Until the engine computes the ratio from the planned count, the step data carries the rounded estimate from the synthetic plan. Since 2026-10-14 it does (review finding C2.12): a step carries its tool's rate and, where the record gives one, the direct count, and the engine computes round(days × 5 h × 3600 s × rate ÷ planned blows) once the step is planned (`src/timeline/step-info.ts`), so the ratio follows the plan the page actually plays on either figure; the hand-set estimates are gone.
- **Known departures recorded for the next round** (2026-10-11, after review): about seven in ten point blows are the steep bulk cuts, where technique.md has the sculptor's 45° stroke after the first bulk work, because one shown blow stands for a field of real pits; the block starts as a clean box, although Agostino di Duccio had begun the trunk, the lower limbs and the feet in 1464 and the record says a hole was cut between the legs (`david-history.md`, The block); the next choreography round adds a setup step dated 1464 that applies Agostino's roughing before the 1501 calendar, which also makes the knot on the chest a real feature to strike off. Dust puffs for the point and the drill were deferred until the chips read right; they landed on 2026-10-14 (Tools, below).
- **Agostino di Duccio's start opens the making, 18 August 1464 to 30 April 1466** (2026-10-14; the departure above, made). The commission is dated 18 August 1464 and Agostino lost it in December 1466 (`david-history.md`, The block); no source dates his last blows, so his step ends on 30 April 1466, an inference (the spring before the commission lapsed, leaving the rest of 1466 unworked, as its loss suggests). He had begun "only the trunk, lower limbs and feet" and the 1500 inventory calls the block badly blocked out (`technique.md`, stage 0), so the step takes two layers of bulk craters off the front relief from the rock's top to 2.6 m and stops 8 cm above the figure: a lump, not yet a figure. His region stops below the chest, so the knot of 9 September 1501 is not his. The hole between the legs (Vasari: a Maestro Simone; the documents: Agostino) is a second setup step, a cut through the block between the legs at y 1.2 to 1.6 m with a 3 cm margin, the gap's width probed on the David grid (`src/choreography/regions.ts`, HOLE); it is dated to the last day of Agostino's span, an inference, since the record gives it no date and the documents put the roughing in Agostino's hands. The label shows years before 9 September 1501, so neither day is shown.

## Tools

- **One rigid object per tool id** (2026-10-11, from the tools worker's handoff): `polish` is the straw bunch, and the leather pad with tripoli is a prop laid on the bench; the gilding tool is a gilder's tip with a leaf, wholly inferred, because the record only says the gilding must arrive through a tool; the layout stick is charcoal (the note allows chalk or charcoal); one 1.2 kg iron mallet serves every chisel although the note infers a lighter one for fine claw work, since the contract has one mallet. A second mallet id is the change if a reviewer finds the fine-claw strikes read too heavy.
- **The real-blows ratio assumes an eight-hour working day** (2026-10-11): the catalog's `blowsPerShown` for struck chisels is the note's inferred rhythm (point 1.5 a second, toothed chisels 2) over the 865 documented days at eight hours, divided by the 20,000 shown blows. The note gives no working day; the label says "about".
- **Chips land on the yard floor only** (2026-10-11): the chip contract has no stock surface, so a chip thrown onto an upward-facing surface of the block falls through it. Acceptable for a time-lapse; a stock-aware floor needs the stock sampler in the contract and is deferred.
- **The drill may go straight in for single holes** (2026-10-11): the documented 35 to 45 degrees is the running drill; the pupils and single deep holes are drilled along the surface normal, an inference.
- **Dust puffs at the point and the drill only** (2026-10-14, page-4): technique.md says only that the dust is white and fine and that a handful lay on the scaffold planks; the puff's size, speed and life are inferred. The point throws six grains off the face along its normal at 0.25 to 0.6 m/s, slowed by the air, swelling from about 1.5 to 14 cm and gone within 0.9 s; the drill lets out four smaller, slower grains. White on lit marble the puff barely shows, as real dust does; it reads against the shadows and the yard. The claws, the flat, rasps, files and abrasives puff nothing: their powder stays in the marks, and a haze at every stroke would hide the finishing work. The dust settles nowhere: like the chips it has no stock surface to land on.
- **The webs are taken by the dente di cane after its bands, and the drill rows between the legs run in the crease under the crotch** (2026-10-14, engine-a). The record says the fine claw works round the form until the sides and back are level with the front (technique.md, stage 4), and infers the drill "around the stump and between the legs where the chisel cannot reach", the webbing between its holes knocked out with a narrow chisel or left (stage 5; [S18]); it does not say which tool took the stone the bands passed over in the hollows and the gaps between two parts. Choice: a step after fine-claw-all, `fine-claw-gaps`, with the same dente di cane at the same 1.5 mm margin, worked into each web from its open side with the shaft clear of stone (`src/choreography/generators/webs.ts`), its shown blow the combing patch that stands for many strokes (`src/stock/tool-volumes.ts`, PATCH_SIZE_M); not the point, which Cellini stops half a finger off the skin and which bruises where it strikes (technique.md, How marble behaves), nor the roundel, whose 9 mm scoop would need thousands of shown blows for the 3,718 cells the fine claw left on the David. A web no chisel reaches with its shaft in air is left standing. Evidence: on the David at 8 mm the stone farther than 9.5 mm from the figure at t = N fell from 340 cells in 39 clusters (between the curls, in pockets of the stump) to none, for 561 shown blows of the new step (`src/choreography/david-steps.slow.test.ts`). The drill rows: a probe of the David grid found the thighs about 16 cm apart at y 2.0 and wider below, and the crease under the crotch 8 to 56 mm wide from y 2.12 to 2.30; two running rows down it (`LEG_GAPS`, `src/choreography/landmarks.ts`) drill its walls through the margin's skin in a step of their own, `between-the-legs`, since the dente di cane has already opened it; the gap of one or two cells between the stump and the calf stays with fingers-sling-stump.

- **The scaffold stands 0.35 m clear of the block, with plank bridges laid when the work needs them** (2026-10-11, after review; `src/tools/scaffold.ts`). Decks at 1.5 and 3.2 m on the front and the sides, 0.8 m wide, a top beam at 5.85 m; a head bridge of two planks across the front at 3.2 m with its near edge 0.6 m from the block's centre plane, and back bridges at both levels just behind the block, which technique.md stage 0's "workmen and timber" allows and the record does not describe. Reach from a platform's edge: 2.35 m up (shoulder 1.4, arm 0.7, tool 0.25), 0.9 m down kneeling, 1.0 m out (arm, tool, a 5 cm lean). Why 0.35 m, not the first 0.5: with 0.5 m the figure's front, 0.45 to 0.6 m behind the block face, was out of every deck's reach; 0.35 m is a working scaffold's distance from a wall. A carry between the bench and the stone is capped at 2.5 s however long its route: in the time-lapse a longer route is walked faster. The page lays a bridge only from the first step with a blow that no deck or the floor reaches and whose nearest platform it is, and never while stone fills its planks (2026-10-14, page-4; `src/viewer/bridges.ts`): the head bridge's near edge lies 17 cm inside the uncarved block's front, and drawn from the start it pierced the stone. Measured at 8 mm on the David and the synthetic figure alike: the head bridge is first needed by step 4 (`chest-and-face`), the upper back bridge by step 8 (`sides-point`), the lower back bridge by none, and each one's box is clear of stone when it is first needed.

## Page

- **The plank enclosure is cut away from outside** (2026-10-11, from the page worker's handoff): the turata's planks face inward only, so the back, left and right presets see the block through the walls while their posts and rails stay visible. The record gives no wall height, and a closed shed would blind three presets.
- **Video frames go to ffmpeg as JPEG** (2026-10-11): Playwright's bundled ffmpeg is built without a PNG decoder or the image2 demuxer, so `scripts/export-video.mjs` pipes JPEG frames (quality 95) through image2pipe; engine-options.md section 7's PNG command cannot run on that binary.
- **The carry from the bench is a time stretch** (2026-10-11): rather than extra blows, the first blow of a step holds its approach window for the tools' `approachSeconds` during forward playback, so the tool crosses the yard at a hand's speed; nothing changes at integer `t`. A tool the next step keeps is not set down between the two steps (2026-10-13, after review): the making hands the cycle the earlier step's last blow as `previous` and, at the integer between them, holds the tool where that blow left it, so the pose is continuous across the boundary; the review had measured a 3.8 m jump to the bench at `t = 3`.

## Figure

- **The figure is the Scan the World model from Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0** (2026-10-11). `mesh-sources.md` finds it the only full-figure David with a stated open licence, a stated size and checksum, and a direct URL; its provenance is a composite of photogrammetry of the marble and a structured-light scan of a plaster cast head, which the note records, and a cast's shape differs from the marble by less than the grid resolves. The mesh is fetched by the pipeline into an ignored folder and never committed; the attribution appears in the page footer and in the grid header.
